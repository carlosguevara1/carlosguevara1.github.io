I am a Peruvian economist currently pursuing the Master of Arts in Economics (MAE) at the University of Chicago.

Before beginning the MAE, I worked as a Research Analyst on the United States Desk at the International Monetary Fund, where I contributed to research and policy work on the U.S. economy. Earlier, I worked at the Inter-American Development Bank and the Central Bank of Peru.

I also contribute to open-source econometrics. I am co-author of csdid, the Python implementation of the staggered difference-in-differences estimator of Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021). My contributions include treatment-effect aggregation and visualization tools. The package has now surpassed <strong>400,000 downloads</strong>.

I was born in Cusco and grew up in Peru, where I studied Economics at the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú. I am particularly proud to be the first in my family to graduate from university. That background has shaped both my ambitions and the way I approach my work.

Beyond economics, I enjoy photography, salsa dancing, international food, and movies. I am also endlessly inspired by the work ethic of Cristiano Ronaldo, the economics of Paul Samuelson, and the creativity of Quentin Tarantino.
